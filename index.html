Key Ingredients in VittaBurn

The following are examples of ingredients commonly found in weight-management supplements. They are not a verified ingredient list for VittaBurn. Check the manufacturer's label before using this section as product-specific content.

1

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract contains catechins, including EGCG, and may also contain caffeine. Researchers have studied these compounds for their potential effects on energy expenditure and fat metabolism. Any weight-management effects tend to be modest, and concentrated extracts can cause side effects, including liver problems in some cases.
2

Green Coffee Bean Extract

Green coffee bean extract contains chlorogenic acids and is commonly used in weight-management products. Some research has examined its possible effects on body weight and glucose metabolism, but evidence remains limited and does not establish that every supplement containing it is effective.
3

Garcinia Cambogia

Garcinia cambogia is a tropical fruit extract used in some weight-management supplements. It contains hydroxycitric acid (HCA), which has been studied for its possible effects on appetite and fat metabolism. Evidence of meaningful weight loss is weak, and some products containing Garcinia cambogia have been associated with liver injury.
4

Caffeine

Caffeine is a stimulant found naturally in coffee, tea, and several plant extracts. It can temporarily increase alertness and may modestly increase energy expenditure. However, excessive intake can cause sleep disturbances, anxiety, palpitations, and other unwanted effects.
5

L-Carnitine

L-carnitine helps transport fatty acids into mitochondria, where they can be used in energy production. It is included in some fitness and weight-management supplements, although evidence for significant weight loss in otherwise healthy adults is limited.
6

Capsaicin

Capsaicin is the compound responsible for the heat of chili peppers. It has been studied for its potential effects on thermogenesis and appetite. Any effect on weight management is likely to be modest, and concentrated products may cause digestive discomfort.
7

Chromium

Chromium is an essential trace mineral involved in normal nutrient metabolism. Some supplements include it to support glucose metabolism, but evidence that chromium supplementation produces meaningful weight loss is limited. Excessive intake may be harmful.
8

Apple Cider Vinegar Powder

Apple cider vinegar powder is used in some wellness supplements. Research has explored its potential effects on appetite and blood glucose, but evidence for sustained weight loss remains limited. It should not be treated as a substitute for balanced nutrition.
